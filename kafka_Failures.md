consumer slowly falls behind.A bad partition key creates uneven load.A rebalance keeps interrupting processing.Offsets get committed at the wrong point.A broker starts running out of disk.Retries quietly introduce duplicate processing.And suddenly, what looked perfectly fine in lower environments becomes a production issue. 🚨Some of the Kafka failure patterns I usually pay the most attention to:🔹 Consumer LagConsumers are processing slower than messages are arriving.→ Scale consumers, review partition count, and optimize processing logic.🔹 Frequent RebalancingToo many rebalances can keep interrupting message processing.→ Check consumer stability, processing time, max.poll.interval.ms, session settings, and unnecessary restarts.🔹 Duplicate ProcessingRetries can cause the same event to be processed more than once.→ Producer idempotence helps, but downstream processing should also be idempotent where needed.🔹 Partition SkewOne or two partitions become overloaded while others stay relatively idle.→ Revisit the partition key and distribution strategy.🔹 Offset ManagementWhere and when you commit offsets matters.→ A wrong commit strategy can lead to either reprocessing or missing work.🔹 Disk & Retention PressureRetention settings that look harmless initially can eventually fill broker storage.→ Monitor disk usage and align retention with the actual business requirement.🔹 Under-Replicated PartitionsUsually a sign that something deeper is happening.→ Check broker health, disk, network, ISR, and replication status.🔹 Ordering IssuesKafka guarantees ordering within a partition, not across the entire topic.→ Your partitioning strategy matters more than many teams realize.And one important point 👇A broker going down does not automatically mean Kafka becomes unavailable.With proper replication, healthy ISR, and balanced leadership, Kafka is designed to tolerate broker failures.That’s where production experience really starts to matter.It’s easy to learn the happy path:Producer → Topic → ConsumerBut real Kafka knowledge comes from understanding what happens when consumers slow down, brokers disappear, retries kick in, or traffic stops behaving the way
